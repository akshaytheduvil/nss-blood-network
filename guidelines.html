SECTION 01

Who Can Donate?

Basic eligibility for whole-blood donation in India. Final eligibility is always confirmed by the authorized blood bank's medical officer.

✓ You can donate if…

  • You are between 18 and 65 years old
  • Your body weight is at least 50 kg
  • Your haemoglobin is at least 12.5 g/dL
  • Your pulse and blood pressure are within normal range
  • You have not donated blood in the last 90 days
  • You are in good general health and feel well
  • You have no active infection or fever

✕ Temporarily postpone if…

  • You have a cold, flu, or fever
  • You took antibiotics in the last 48 hours
  • You had a tattoo or piercing in the last 6 months
  • You are pregnant or breastfeeding
  • You had major surgery in the last 6 months
  • You are menstruating heavily today
  • You drank alcohol in the last 24 hours
ℹ️

These are general guidelines. Some medical conditions may require a longer deferral period. Always inform the blood bank about any medication or health condition.

SECTION 02

Before You Donate.

A little preparation goes a long way. Follow these simple tips in the 24 hours leading up to your donation.

24 hrs before

💧 Hydrate Well

Drink plenty of water and fluids. Being well-hydrated makes the process smoother.

24 hrs before

🚫 No Alcohol

Avoid alcohol completely — it dehydrates the body and can affect your vitals.

Night before

😴 Good Sleep

Aim for 7–8 hours of quality sleep. Rest helps your body handle the donation better.

3 hrs before

🍽️ Eat a Meal

Have a healthy, low-fat meal. Avoid fatty foods which can cloud the blood plasma.

On the day

🪪 Carry ID

Bring a valid photo ID (Aadhaar, college ID, or driving licence) for registration.

On the day

👕 Wear Comfortable Clothes

Choose a shirt with sleeves that can be easily rolled up above the elbow.

SECTION 03

The Donation Process.

From walking in to walking out — here's exactly what happens during a whole-blood donation.

1

Registration

You'll fill a short form with your name, age, contact details, and a brief medical questionnaire.

≈ 5 minutes
2

Health Check

A medical officer checks your weight, blood pressure, pulse, and haemoglobin level. A drop of blood is tested.

≈ 5–10 minutes
3

Donation

You relax on a comfortable bed while 350–450 ml of blood is collected. A new, sterile needle is used for every donor.

≈ 8–10 minutes
4

Rest & Refreshments

You rest for 10–15 minutes and are given juice, biscuits, or a light snack to help your body recover.

≈ 15 minutes
✓

Done!

You'll receive a donor card or certificate. Your blood will be tested, processed, and matched to a patient in need.

🌿
SECTION 04

After You Donate.

Your body replaces the plasma within 24 hours and the red blood cells within a few weeks. Follow these tips for a smooth recovery.

🩹

Keep the Bandage On

Leave the pressure bandage on for 4–5 hours. Keep the site dry and clean.

💧

Drink More Fluids

Drink extra water or juice for the next 24 hours to replace lost fluids.

🍎

Eat Well

Have iron-rich foods — leafy greens, dates, jaggery, and lentils.

🚫

Skip Strenuous Activity

Avoid heavy exercise, lifting, or intense sports for the rest of the day.

🚭

No Smoking / Alcohol

Avoid smoking and alcohol for at least 24 hours after donating.

⚠️

Watch for Dizziness

If you feel dizzy, sit or lie down and raise your legs. Contact the blood bank if it continues.

SECTION 05

Myths vs. Facts.

Common misconceptions about blood donation — cleared up.

MYTH

Donating blood makes you weak.

FACT

Your body replenishes the plasma within 24 hours and red blood cells in a few weeks. Rest and fluids are all you need.

MYTH

You can catch diseases from donating.

FACT

A new, sterile, single-use needle is used for every donor. There is zero risk of infection.

MYTH

Donating is very painful.

FACT

You'll feel a small pinch when the needle goes in. The rest of the process is painless.

MYTH

Only young people can donate.

FACT

Anyone aged 18–65 and meeting basic health criteria can donate blood.

MYTH

Donating regularly harms your health.

FACT

With a 90-day gap between donations, regular donating is safe and can even benefit heart health.
